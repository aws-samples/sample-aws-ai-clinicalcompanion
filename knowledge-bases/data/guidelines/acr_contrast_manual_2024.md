## 1. Introduction and Scope

The ACR Manual on Contrast Media provides evidence-based guidance for the safe and effective use of contrast media in diagnostic imaging. This manual is updated regularly based on the latest scientific evidence and clinical experience.

**Target Audience:**
- Radiologists
- Referring physicians
- Nurses and technologists
- Healthcare administrators

**Evidence Levels:**
- **Level A:** Strong scientific evidence from multiple RCTs
- **Level B:** Moderate evidence from observational studies
- **Level C:** Expert consensus opinion

## 2. Iodinated Contrast Media

### 2.1 Types of Iodinated Contrast

**High-Osmolar Contrast Media (HOCM):**
- Rarely used today due to higher adverse event rates
- Osmolality: 1400-2400 mOsm/kg

**Low-Osmolar Contrast Media (LOCM):**
- Standard for most applications
- Osmolality: 600-900 mOsm/kg
- Examples: Iopamidol, Iohexol, Ioversol

**Iso-Osmolar Contrast Media (IOCM):**
- Osmolality similar to blood (290 mOsm/kg)
- Example: Iodixanol
- May have lower CI-AKI risk in high-risk patients

### 2.2 Renal Safety of Iodinated Contrast

**Risk Stratification by eGFR:**

| eGFR (mL/min/1.73m²) | Risk Category | ACR Recommendation |
|----------------------|---------------|-------------------|
| ≥60 | Normal/Low Risk | Standard protocol, no special precautions |
| 45-59 | Mild Risk | Consider hydration, use LOCM/IOCM |
| 30-44 | Moderate Risk | Enhanced precautions, consider alternatives |
| <30 | High Risk | Avoid if possible, nephrology consultation |

**Evidence Level:** A

### 2.3 Contrast-Induced Acute Kidney Injury (CI-AKI)

**Definition:** Increase in serum creatinine ≥0.5 mg/dL or ≥25% from baseline within 48-72 hours of contrast administration.

**Risk Factors:**
- Pre-existing renal impairment (eGFR <60)
- Diabetes mellitus
- Dehydration
- Heart failure
- Age >70 years
- Multiple myeloma
- Concurrent nephrotoxic medications

**Prevention Strategies:**

1. **Hydration (Most Important):**
   - IV isotonic saline 1 mL/kg/hour for 6-12 hours before and after contrast
   - Oral hydration acceptable for low-risk patients
   - **Evidence Level:** A

2. **Minimize Contrast Volume:**
   - Use lowest volume necessary for diagnostic quality
   - Consider contrast volume to eGFR ratio <3
   - **Evidence Level:** B

3. **Avoid Nephrotoxic Medications:**
   - Hold NSAIDs 24 hours before and after
   - Hold diuretics if possible
   - **Evidence Level:** C

4. **Use LOCM or IOCM:**
   - Preferred over HOCM in all patients
   - IOCM may have slight advantage in high-risk patients
   - **Evidence Level:** A

## Section 4.2: Renal Considerations for Gadolinium-Based Contrast Agents

### Overview
Gadolinium-based contrast agents (GBCAs) are generally safe for patients with normal kidney function. However, patients with reduced kidney function require special consideration.

### Kidney Function Assessment
- **eGFR ≥ 60 mL/min/1.73m²**: Normal kidney function - GBCAs can be used without restriction
- **eGFR 45-59 mL/min/1.73m²**: Mildly reduced - GBCAs can be used with caution
- **eGFR 30-44 mL/min/1.73m²**: Moderately reduced (Stage 3b CKD) - GBCAs are **relatively contraindicated**
- **eGFR < 30 mL/min/1.73m²**: Severely reduced - GBCAs should be avoided

### Recommendations for eGFR 30-44 mL/min/1.73m²

At eGFR 30-44 mL/min/1.73m², gadolinium-based contrast agents are **relatively contraindicated**. This means:

1. **Risk-Benefit Assessment Required**: The clinical benefit must clearly outweigh the risk
2. **Use Lowest Effective Dose**: If contrast is deemed essential, use the minimum dose necessary
3. **Prefer Macrocyclic Agents**: Use macrocyclic GBCAs (gadobutrol, gadoterate) over linear agents
4. **Consider Alternatives**: Evaluate non-contrast imaging sequences when possible

### Alternative Imaging Approaches

For patients with eGFR 30-44, consider:
- **Non-contrast MRI sequences**: STIR, T1-weighted, T2-weighted
- **Alternative modalities**: CT without contrast, ultrasound, nuclear medicine
- **Delayed imaging**: If contrast is essential, consider delayed imaging after kidney function improves

### Evidence Level
**Level A**: Based on strong scientific evidence from multiple randomized controlled trials

## 4. Metformin Management

### 4.1 Background

Metformin is a biguanide antidiabetic medication that can cause lactic acidosis in the setting of acute kidney injury. Contrast-induced AKI could theoretically precipitate metformin-associated lactic acidosis.

### 4.2 ACR Recommendations

**For Patients with Normal Renal Function (eGFR ≥60):**
- No need to discontinue metformin before or after contrast
- **Evidence Level:** A

**For Patients with eGFR 45-59:**
- Hold metformin at time of contrast administration
- Measure creatinine 48 hours post-contrast
- Resume metformin if creatinine stable
- **Evidence Level:** B

**For Patients with eGFR 30-44:**
- Hold metformin 48 hours before contrast
- Measure creatinine 48 hours post-contrast
- Resume metformin only if creatinine stable or improved
- **Evidence Level:** B

**For Patients with eGFR <30:**
- Metformin should already be discontinued per endocrine guidelines
- If contrast is essential, coordinate with endocrinology
- **Evidence Level:** C

## 5. Allergy and Hypersensitivity Reactions

### 5.1 Classification of Reactions

**Immediate Reactions (within 1 hour):**
- Mild: Urticaria, itching, flushing
- Moderate: Bronchospasm, facial edema
- Severe: Anaphylaxis, laryngeal edema, cardiovascular collapse

**Delayed Reactions (1 hour to 7 days):**
- Skin rashes
- Flu-like symptoms
- Rarely: Stevens-Johnson syndrome

### 5.2 Risk Factors for Contrast Reactions

**High Risk:**
- Previous moderate/severe contrast reaction (risk increases 5-fold)
- Asthma requiring medication
- Multiple drug allergies

**Moderate Risk:**
- Previous mild contrast reaction
- Shellfish/iodine allergy (controversial - likely not true cross-reactivity)
- Atopic conditions

### 5.3 Premedication Protocol

**For High-Risk Patients:**

**Option 1 (Preferred - 13-hour protocol):**
- Prednisone 50 mg PO at 13 hours, 7 hours, and 1 hour before contrast
- Diphenhydramine 50 mg IV/IM/PO 1 hour before contrast

**Option 2 (Emergency - 5-hour protocol):**
- Methylprednisolone 40 mg IV or Hydrocortisone 200 mg IV every 4 hours (2 doses)
- Diphenhydramine 50 mg IV 1 hour before contrast

**Evidence Level:** B

**Note:** Premedication reduces but does not eliminate risk of reactions.

## 6. Special Populations

### 6.1 Pregnancy and Lactation

**Iodinated Contrast:**
- Category B (no evidence of fetal harm in animal studies)
- Use only if benefit outweighs risk
- Inform patient of theoretical risks
- **Evidence Level:** C

**Gadolinium Contrast:**
- Crosses placenta and enters fetal circulation
- Avoid unless absolutely necessary
- Prefer non-contrast imaging
- **Evidence Level:** C

**Breastfeeding:**
- <0.01% of maternal dose excreted in breast milk
- Breastfeeding can continue after contrast (both iodinated and gadolinium)
- **Evidence Level:** A

### 6.2 Pediatric Patients

**Renal Function:**
- Children generally have lower CI-AKI risk than adults
- eGFR testing not routinely required for children with no risk factors
- **Evidence Level:** B

**Contrast Dosing:**
- Weight-based dosing: 1.5-2 mL/kg for iodinated contrast
- Maximum dose: 150 mL regardless of weight
- **Evidence Level:** C

### 6.3 Elderly Patients (>70 years)

**Considerations:**
- Higher baseline CI-AKI risk
- Often have multiple comorbidities
- May have reduced renal reserve despite normal creatinine
- Consider eGFR testing even if creatinine appears normal
- **Evidence Level:** B

## 7. Alternative Imaging Strategies

### 7.1 When to Consider Alternatives

**Indications for Alternative Imaging:**
- eGFR <30 (high CI-AKI risk)
- Previous severe contrast reaction despite premedication
- Pregnancy (avoid radiation and gadolinium)
- Patient refusal of contrast

### 7.2 Alternative Modalities by Clinical Indication

**Spinal Imaging:**
- **Alternative:** MRI without contrast
- **Diagnostic Accuracy:** Excellent for most spinal pathology
- **Limitations:** May miss subtle infections or tumors

**Abdominal Imaging:**
- **Alternative:** Ultrasound, MRI without contrast
- **Diagnostic Accuracy:** Good for many conditions
- **Limitations:** Operator-dependent (US), longer scan time (MRI)

**Vascular Imaging:**
- **Alternative:** MR angiography (non-contrast techniques: TOF, PC)
- **Diagnostic Accuracy:** Good for large vessels
- **Limitations:** Lower resolution than CTA

**Pulmonary Embolism:**
- **Alternative:** V/Q scan
- **Diagnostic Accuracy:** Moderate (many indeterminate results)
- **Limitations:** Less sensitive than CTPA

## 8. Quality Improvement and Safety Monitoring

### 8.1 Institutional Requirements

**ACR Recommendations:**
- Track all contrast administrations
- Monitor CI-AKI rates quarterly
- Review adverse events monthly
- Maintain emergency medications and equipment
- Train staff annually on contrast safety

### 8.2 Key Performance Indicators

**Metrics to Monitor:**
- CI-AKI rate (target: <2% for low-risk patients)
- Contrast reaction rate (target: <0.5% for immediate reactions)
- Premedication compliance for high-risk patients
- Metformin hold compliance
- Hydration protocol compliance

## 9. Summary of Key Recommendations

### Iodinated Contrast
✅ **eGFR ≥60:** Standard protocol, no special precautions  
✅ **eGFR 45-59:** Consider hydration, use LOCM/IOCM  
✅ **eGFR 30-44:** Enhanced precautions, consider alternatives  
✅ **eGFR <30:** Avoid if possible, nephrology consultation

### Gadolinium Contrast
✅ **eGFR ≥60:** No restrictions  
✅ **eGFR 45-59:** Use with caution  
✅ **eGFR 30-44:** Relatively contraindicated, use lowest dose if essential  
✅ **eGFR <30:** Avoid

### Metformin
✅ **eGFR ≥60:** No need to hold  
✅ **eGFR 45-59:** Hold at time of contrast, check creatinine at 48h  
✅ **eGFR 30-44:** Hold 48h before, check creatinine at 48h  
✅ **eGFR <30:** Should already be discontinued

### Allergy Prevention
✅ **High-risk patients:** Premedicate with corticosteroids + antihistamines  
✅ **Previous severe reaction:** Consider alternative imaging
